# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Primary: recruiters, hiring managers and product/engineering leaders evaluating Ashfaque for senior AI Product Manager, Technical Product Manager, AI Product Owner and AI strategy roles. They arrive from a LinkedIn message, a recruiter email or a résumé link, usually on a laptop between meetings or on a phone in the evening, and decide in under a minute whether he is worth a call.

Secondary: senior stakeholders and executives doing a deeper read before an interview loop. They open one case study and read it properly, looking for judgment, trade-offs and evidence.

## Product Purpose

A career asset, not an online résumé. The site positions Ashfaque as an AI Product Leader who builds AI-powered products that solve real business problems and move measurable outcomes.

Within ten seconds a visitor should know:

- **Who:** AI Product Leader (Engineer → Digital Product / Consulting → Enterprise AI Product)
- **What:** enterprise AI and digital products at telecom scale
- **How:** product strategy + technical depth + execution
- **Proof:** large-scale products, measurable outcomes, innovation awards, an invention disclosure

Success: the visitor downloads the résumé, opens a case study, or makes contact. The site should read as "here is a person who can own an AI product", never "a business analyst with a list of technologies".

## Brand Personality

Precise, candid, quietly confident. Senior without being stiff.

- Voice: specific nouns and verbs, real numbers with their source, trade-offs stated plainly. No hype vocabulary, no "passionate about", no emoji.
- Emotional goal: trust. The visitor should feel they are reading the working notes of someone who has shipped, measured and learned.
- Physical references: an engineering drawing's title block, a precision instrument's faceplate, a well-kept product spec.

## Anti-references

- Generic developer portfolios: skill-logo walls, "Hi, I'm…" heroes, typewriter effects, neon gradients, particle fields, 3D AI brains, stock "futuristic" imagery.
- Corporate-blue LinkedIn clones.
- SaaS landing-page templates: gradient text, glass cards everywhere, identical icon-card grids, a tracked uppercase eyebrow above every section.
- Anything that makes him look like he is applying for a frontend role (CSS showing off, 17 animation effects, gimmicks that delay content).

## Design Principles

1. **Evidence over adjectives.** Every claim carries a number, a source or an artifact. If a fact is not verified, it is not published (placeholders are dev-only).
2. **Show how he thinks.** Case studies expose decisions, trade-offs and lessons, not just outcomes.
3. **Product first, technology second.** AI is presented as a means; the problem and the metric lead.
4. **Restraint is the premium signal.** One accent, generous space, motion only where it explains something.
5. **Fast and honest.** Prerendered pages, no layout shift, graceful fallbacks, no dead ends.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Target WCAG 2.2 AA: body text ≥ 4.5:1, large text and UI ≥ 3:1, visible focus on every interactive element.
- Full keyboard navigation, skip link, semantic landmarks, one `h1` per page, labelled figures (diagrams have text equivalents).
- `prefers-reduced-motion` disables all non-essential motion; content is never gated behind an animation.
- Mobile is first-class: thumb-sized targets (≥ 44px), no horizontal overflow, readable type at 320px.
